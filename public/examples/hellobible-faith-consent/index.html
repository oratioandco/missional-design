A separate consent for using faith data with a partner AI

Before an account is created, “We protect your privacy!” shows two unticked boxes: accepting the Terms of Use and Privacy Policy, and agreeing “that HelloBible may use what I share about my faith to personalize my answers, generated by a partner AI.” Below: “Your conversations stay private. They are never sold or used for advertising, and you can delete them at any time.”

Design takeaway

A separate, unticked consent for faith data matches GDPR’s explicit consent for religious belief. Name the AI partner and where data is processed, and check that the app still works for someone who declines the second box.

107 · Habits and spiritual formation

Check-ins and assessments

From a few setup questions that only shape what the app suggests, through a daily check-in, a gifts inventory and a church survey, to a spiritual score as a warning case: decide what each question is for, what is kept, who sees a result, and how much the result claims to know.

Research-informed design proposal101 · Conversation and content

How human the assistant seems

From a plain tool with no name, through labelled and named assistants, to personas, companions and chatbots speaking as Jesus: decide how much personhood an AI in a faith app performs, and make what it is as clear as how it sounds.

Research-informed design proposal109 · Conversation and content

AI memory

From an assistant that keeps nothing, through context the person adds and a conversation they can see, to memory they switch on and can see item by item, and a profile the app builds from their prayers as a warning case: decide what the assistant may use, what it keeps, and whether the person can see and change it.

Research-informed design proposal128 · Conversation and content

Meeting the person where they are

From the same answer for everyone, through reading the message in front of it, asking before assuming and preferences the person sets, to assumptions shown within one conversation and a remembered profile of someone’s faith: decide how the assistant tells a sceptic from a believer, and what it may keep about where they stand.
