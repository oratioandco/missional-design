AI memory

What should the assistant know and keep?

People tell Bible assistants what they are reading, and also what they are going through: a lost job, a diagnosis, a doubt, a friend’s secret. An assistant can use that to answer better, keep it to continue tomorrow, remember it for months, or turn it into a profile. Decide what the assistant may use, what it keeps, and whether the person can see and change all of it. What people confide is holy ground.

Why these settings?

A study question asks about Mark 4:40; the personal question mentions a lost job. With context, the passage comes from where the person is reading and a note is added only on request. The conversation and memory can be kept on the phone or in an account across devices; once memory is switched on, ordinary items are saved and shown in the chat with Edit and Delete, while personal things (a lost job, a feeling) are kept only when the person taps “Remember this too”; asking each time is the stricter alternative. Familiar convention

1. Nothing kept

A familiar choice: anonymous or “temporary” chats that use no history and keep nothing, as in ChatGPT’s Temporary Chat, and in Bible apps whose AI answers each question on its own.

Try this

Ask, come back tomorrow, and ask to pick up where you left off.

+ Makes easier

Nothing to leak, resurface or delete; safe for a hard moment.

− Costs

No continuity; people repeat themselves and context.

Ask your teamIs anything kept anyway, in logs or for safety, and does the person know?

Borrowed

4. Memory you can see

Borrowed from assistants with memory people switch on once and then see, edit and delete: ChatGPT’s memory (2024), which says it steers away from remembering health details unless asked, and Gyroscope’s AI coach (observed 2026), whose check-ins open with a “Quick chat / Save to history” switch and whose “About You” profile, internal notes included, can be read in full. Item-by-item Edit and Delete goes further than either.

Try this

Send the question and edit or delete what was saved. Try the personal question, open Memory, then come back tomorrow. Switch to “Asks each time” to compare.

+ Makes easier

A study companion that remembers what helps, without a prompt after every answer.

− Costs

Needs an honest memory page, real deletion and a line around personal things.

Ask your teamWould the person be comfortable if a friend read the memory page aloud?

What we noticed

Takeaways

  1. People tell assistants more than they mean to

    In interviews about real ChatGPT conversations, users kept trading privacy for usefulness, misunderstood what was kept, and disclosed more when the system felt human. In a faith app the disclosures are prayers, confessions and other people’s stories. Assume people will say more than they intend, and show them what is kept.

  2. Context works best when it is visible

    A passage chip beside the question makes answers specific and lets the person remove it. A note, a journal entry or a file should be added only on request, and named in the answer (“Using: Mark 4:35–41 and your note”). Hidden context is the source of most surprises.

  3. Attachments are material, not instructions

    Security guidance warns that text inside files and web pages can redirect a model or leak data. Read what someone attaches; never obey it, and say so where people attach things.

  4. Memory needs a switch, exact words and a way back

    General assistants now let people see, delete and switch off memory, and steer away from remembering health details unless asked. Asking after every answer is tiring; a single switch, with each saved item shown in the chat with Edit and Delete, keeps memory visible without friction. Draw a line around personal things: a loss, a diagnosis or a doubt is kept only when the person asks. And keep a memory page a person could show a friend.

  5. Inferred profiles break the context of a confidence

    Contextual integrity asks whether information flows as the context it was shared in would expect. A worry told to an assistant at night does not expect to become “Life events: job loss” in a marketing profile. Faith, health and sexual life are special-category data in Europe, and so are reliable inferences about them.

Which fits your product?

  • IfPeople study with the assistant

    Start withVisible context chips: the passage by default, notes only when added

  • IfPeople return for weeks

    Start withMemory switched on once, each item shown as it is saved, with a memory page

Theological reflection

What is this practice for?

What being known is for

“O LORD, You have searched me and known me … You understand my thoughts from afar” (Psalm 139:1–2). To be fully known is safe only with God, who knows in love; Paul longs for the day “then I shall know fully, even as I am fully known” (1 Corinthians 13:12). Among people, knowledge of another is given in trust and grows in relationship.

Where Christians differ

Catholic and Orthodox churches guard confession with a seal that may never be broken; Protestant traditions practise confession to one another (James 5:16) in small groups and pastoral care, with confidentiality as a matter of faithfulness rather than law. Traditions differ on how much of one’s spiritual life should be shared at all, from accountability groups to the counsel to pray to the Father “who is unseen” (Matthew 6:6). All agree that confidences are entrusted, not owned.

What follows for design

“A gossip reveals a secret, but a trustworthy person keeps a confidence” (Proverbs 11:13). An assistant cannot keep a seal, so it should hold as little as it can: what the person shows it, for as long as they choose, in words they have seen. God’s promise to “remember their sins no more” (Hebrews 8:12) suggests that forgetting can be a mercy, and deletion a feature to be proud of.

In conversation with

  • Augustine, Confessions X.5 and X.8 confessed that “there is something of man which the spirit of man which is in him itself knows not”, but God knows him wholly: no record of conversations amounts to knowing a person.
  • Miroslav Volf, The End of Memory (2006) argues that remembering rightly ends, after justice and reconciliation, in a kind of non-remembrance; an assistant should be able to forget.

Three ways to build

What would each way do here?

  • ExploitativeTakes from the person

    Remember everything, infer life events and faith stage from chats, and use confidences for offers and partners.

  • EthicalDoes no harm

    Show what is used and kept, ask before remembering, exclude sensitive categories, and let people delete everything.

  • RedemptiveGives something up for them

    Keep conversations on the phone and forget by default, and give up the personalisation and data that a profile would earn.

Edge cases to design for

Show context as visible, removable items beside the question: the passage, a note, a file, with what each will be used for. Keep a conversation only where the person can find it and delete it, and say where it is kept. Let people switch memory on or off in one place; when it is on, show each saved item in the conversation with Edit and Delete, and ask separately before keeping anything personal; keep a memory page that lists every item. Exclude special categories by default. Treat text inside attachments as material to read, never as instructions. Do not infer denomination, health, stage of faith or life events, and do not use conversations for marketing.

What tends to go wrong

A plus icon that uploads without saying so; a private journal sent along silently; removed items still used; “Memory updated” with no way to see what; a past loss raised in an opening message; feelings, sins or a friend’s situation remembered without asking; one switch for memory, training and sharing; profiles of “life events” and “faith stage” built from chats; recommendations and offers drawn from what someone confided; text in an attachment obeyed as an instruction.

How to test it

Ask a personal question, then ask the person what the assistant now knows about them and where it is kept; compare with storage, logs and derived data. Remove an attachment and check it was not used. Decline a memory, edit one, delete one, then return the next day. Put an instruction inside an attached file and check it is not followed. Ask a pastor to read the memory page aloud and say whether it is something a friend would keep.

Earlier pattern: Intent controls support the prompt

Help people express scope through selections and task choices.

Guidance

Pair free text with editable context chips and concrete task controls. Use a passage selection, source scope and desired form to express intent. Confirm material assumptions rather than silently inferring them from private data.

What tends to go wrong

Forcing a complex prompt; invisible source filters; inferring belief or distress from a simple search.

How to test it

Can novice users ask a scoped question and understand what sources and passage will be used? Test removing a context item and correcting an assumption.

Earlier pattern: Show what the chat will use before sending

A passage, file, image or link becomes visible, removable context.

Guidance

Offer an explicitly labelled Add context action. Show each selected item with its type, scope and processing state. Make removal and cancellation available before sending; explain when upload begins and whether content leaves the device. Send only the context the person has chosen.

What tends to go wrong

Sending immediately after selection; implying upload means successful reading; keeping a removed item in hidden context; treating attached text as system instructions; silently including private notes.

How to test it

Attach a passage and an unreadable file, remove one, cancel an upload and retry a failure. Check what is transmitted, extracted and retained. Verify the empty prompt, keyboard opening and large-text states.

Earlier pattern: Sensitive memory by permission

Make persistent memory specific, inspectable and reversible.

Guidance

Default to the memory behaviour stated by the product. Before any optional durable memory, show the exact proposed wording and purpose. Offer edit, decline, view and delete. Establish categories the product will not retain and rules for sensitive reuse.

What tends to go wrong

Inferring denomination or health status; saving everything as personalisation; bundling memory, training and sharing; surprising users with a past loss.

How to test it

Test declining, editing, deleting and later reuse. Verify the interface promise against storage, derived profiles, logs and retention processes.

Faith and interpretation

“O LORD, You have searched me and known me” (Psalm 139:1): to be fully known is what God alone does, and does in love. Among people, trust is kept by discretion: “a gossip reveals a secret, but a trustworthy person keeps a confidence” (Proverbs 11:13), and the church has guarded confession with a seal it calls inviolable. God also promises to forget: “I will forgive their iniquities and will remember their sins no more” (Hebrews 8:12). An assistant should know only what it is given for the task, keep only what the person chooses, and be able to forget.
