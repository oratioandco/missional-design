A design reference

Compare editions across screen sizes

Inspect wording and context while retaining edition identity.

Design proposal · User validation required
Entry

A selected passage and two available editions.

Proposed flow

  1. Select

    Name editions and verify correspondence.

  2. Arrange

    Choose stacked panes, side-by-side panes or verse groups by usable width and task.

  3. Link or explore

    Choose linked verse-anchor scrolling or independent movement.

  4. Inspect

    Open lexical or interlinear detail only when reliable alignment exists.

  5. Rejoin

    Align from a chosen pane, or return to a continuous reading view.

Recovery and alternative routes

Missing, split or merged mappings remain visible. Enlarged text or resizing changes arrangement without resetting the selected verse.

What counts as a complete outcome

The reader can leave with the same passage and chosen edition.

Research-informed design proposal54 · Study and annotations

Linked scrolling follows passage identity

Let parallel panes move together or independently, with an explicit way to rejoin.

Research-informed design proposal55 · Study and annotations

Group editions around a shared verse

Use repeated verse groups when matching corresponding passages matters more than continuous reading.

Research-informed design proposal56 · Study and annotations

Interlinear rows expose their alignment

Distinguish source-language tokens, glosses and translation wording.

Overlaps remain legible

Research-informed design proposal

Engagement facets to specify

Possible situations: Theological and exegetical study, Teaching and preaching preparation. These are overlapping task contexts, not fixed user categories.

Specify the chosen purpose, passage unit, pace, participation, social setting, interpretive task, environment and disclosure or teaching responsibility. Use the scenario framework.
