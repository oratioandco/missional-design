Why a landscape

Across this resource we ask one practical question: when do proven attention, growth and subscription practices fit a faith product, and when do they cross a line? The answer depends on context: the practice, the person, the tradition, and who carries the cost. This landscape shows that context across the field, so your team can see where a product sits and which pressures come with that place.

Read this first

  • A selected landscape, not market share. Products were chosen to show the range of the field, not counted or ranked.
  • Size and position mean nothing. Logo size, order and position never encode quality, adoption or reach. Order within a box is alphabetical.
  • Provider claims, as stated. Every placement rests on the App Store listing or the provider’s own page. Nothing was installed, bought, signed into or tested.
  • Checked 8 November 2026. Listings change, so a product may describe itself differently today.
  • Uneven coverage. The selection leans on the US App Store and is thin outside Europe and North America. A product missing here is unknown to this map, not absent from the field.
  • Logos identify products and imply no endorsement. They remain the property of their owners.

Method

How the landscape was made

Selection

A purposive selection from our app library, a tracked market list and further leads, including European and German-language products on merit, because US ratings underplay them. It is not a census. Giving and church platforms are included as the setting many congregations work in. The unit is a named product, not a company.

Placement

Practice families answer “what does this product help someone do?”. Each product has one editorial main place on the poster and often belongs to several families; the record explains the overlaps. Nested boxes connect related practices. They do not imply ownership, partnership, theological agreement, or a progression from lesser to better practices.

Evidence

Practice-family placements rest on listings and provider pages read between 2 and 8 November 2026; the five views rest on pages read on 8 November 2026. Tradition is never inferred from a name. Not stated means not stated, not neutral. Scale claims such as “#1” or download counts were not used.

Limits

No app workflow, AI answer, privacy behaviour, theological accuracy or spiritual outcome was tested. Being on the map is not a recommendation, and a product’s place says nothing about how well it serves people. For the history behind today’s field, read waves of Bible and faith apps.
