A design reference

Digital spiritual formation

Study how people actually read and practise, and what a design encourages over time.

Practical takeaways

  • Name the chosen practice and its intended benefit before adding reminders, scores or personalisation.
  • Measure activity separately from learning, wellbeing and spiritual formation.
  • Require complete citations, methods, populations and limitations before applying findings from further studies.

Start with the research note

Practice that forms, features that pressure brings together theology of the spiritual disciplines, pastoral writing, habit psychology, health research and consent.

Selected Kairos manuscript review

Tobias Treppmann, Digital Spiritual Formation: The Aperto Vision — A Framework for Spiritual Formation in Digital Culture, Kairos University, March 2026, Draft 4. Selected sections on digital reading, engagement, multimodal practice, commodification and honest assessment were reviewed on 5 October 2026. This unpublished manuscript contributes an analytical framework and theological arguments; its product vision is not an effectiveness study. This record does not represent a review of its entire bibliography. The full manuscript is not published here.

What reviewed sources support

  • Dyer (2019): the reviewed abstract concerns digital Bible reading in a particular evangelical setting. Avoid universal claims about all readers or faith traditions.
  • Clinton (2019): a meta-analysis of 33 experiments found a small paper advantage overall, concentrated in expository text; no reliable narrative difference was found. Medium alone does not settle the best Bible-reader design.
  • Lifeway’s August 2024 survey: among respondents who had read at least part of the Bible (n=1,137), 34% selected systematic reading, 34% suggested passages, 33% need-based lookup and 27% rereading meaningful passages. Multiple answers were allowed. These are overlapping self-reported activities in a weighted US online survey, not mutually exclusive personas or app usage logs.
  • State of the Bible 2025, chapter 3: Bible engagement and flourishing are associated. The report explicitly cautions about causality. This cannot establish that an app feature improves flourishing, or that reading frequency matters more than comprehension.

Four forms of engagement, without a hierarchy

The manuscript proposes a useful analytical vocabulary. A person can move between these forms within a session; they are not faith identities or levels of maturity.

  • Ambient: incidental encounters through a feed, message or shared verse. Show origin and a voluntary route into context.
  • Receptive: a deliberate brief encounter, such as listening to a passage. Reduce setup while preserving choice and a clear ending.
  • Guided: structured reading, reflection or prayer. Explain the practice and tradition; allow skipping, pausing and adapting the sequence.
  • Self-directed: exploration, comparison or study. Preserve navigation, source inspection and working context.

Combine these forms with the engagement facets and task spectrum. Depth, time, expertise, purpose and social context vary independently.

Turn the research into decisions

  • Focused passage or continuous reader? Use a pericope for a bounded reflection, chapter/book reading for continuity, and a study workspace for comparison and preparation. Offer movement between them. Aperto’s passage-centred direction is a product hypothesis to test, not a universal deduction from reading surveys.
  • Audio, text or both? Choose by access needs and setting. Offer transcripts, captions where relevant, speech/music controls and optional screen-off use. Neither a fixed 5–10 minute dose nor superior formation through audio is established by this review.
  • Practice or engagement target? Test understanding, voluntary return and participants’ accounts of usefulness. Keep usage metrics separate from theological judgements. Do not score spiritual maturity from chat.
  • Personalisation or unnecessary disclosure? Ask only what the practice needs; allow a neutral route. Let people choose support rather than infer what they spiritually lack.
  • Portable practice or detached technique? The manuscript’s commodification critique is a theological argument: name a practice’s origin, purpose and tradition, and preserve connections to community. It is not empirical proof that a digital adaptation causes harm.
  • Encouragement or pressure? Review autonomy, shame, competition and interruptions. The behavioural design guide distinguishes mechanisms and evidence; engagement growth alone does not validate formation.

Questions the review leaves open

Test bounded passages against continuous reading with actual tasks; investigate multimodal practice across abilities and traditions; follow voluntary use beyond completion. Review remaining manuscript citations and regional populations before importing statistics, historical market claims or clinical analogies. Preserve disagreement and negative findings. Mindfulness or mental-health research cannot by itself validate an AI devotional.

An open research agenda

This research agenda develops as relevant studies are reviewed. Questions and proposed patterns remain distinct from published findings; new evidence may support, qualify or challenge a design decision.

Actual Bible-reading practices

Research should cover continuous reading, verse lookup, search, thematic exploration, close study, audio, reading alongside sermons, communal practice and movement between print and digital media. Include interruptions, language, translation choices, accessibility and session context. These are research questions, not assumptions that all readers behave the same way.

Gamification and behavioural design

Examine reminders, streaks, completion indicators, rewards and group features against their purpose and people’s lived experience. Study autonomy, motivation, guilt, comparison and returning after a pause. A mechanism that increases app opens may still need separate evaluation for its effect on the practice.

Reading activity and completed plans are observable behaviours. They should not automatically be presented as spiritual growth. Define intended outcomes with appropriate theological framing and participant input, then identify what evidence could support a claim.

How further studies inform the patterns

  1. Record each study’s complete citation, question, publication date, method, participants and setting.
  2. Extract the relevant finding and limitations. Distinguish empirical research, theological argument and design hypothesis.
  3. Link the finding to a pattern or scenario. State whether it motivates, challenges or validates that proposal.
  4. Preserve conflicting results and differences between populations, traditions and contexts.
  5. Revise the card’s evidence status and validation plan. Do not silently relabel an untested proposal as established.

Questions to carry into evaluation

  • Does a progress display acknowledge activity or imply moral standing?
  • Can a plan fit irregular rhythms without punitive backlog?
  • Does returning after a pause feel manageable?
  • Does a reflective step help understanding, or merely collect intimate data?
  • Can reminders be paused without pressure?
  • Can community support practice without ranking private devotion?

The proposed patterns below are hypotheses to investigate. Formation research complements clinical, safeguarding and legal expertise; it does not replace them in sensitive conversations.

Specify the engagement being studied

Reading research and engagement facets distinguish lookup, sustained reading, devotion, contemplation, learning, community, teaching and study. State the sample, task and outcome before applying further formation findings.

Values and formation evidence

Plant and water; leave the growth to God defines a normative question about the good the tool serves. Theological aims, participant accounts, observed behaviour and causal evidence answer different questions. Use the principle review without treating a values statement as an established formation outcome.
