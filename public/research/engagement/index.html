Editorial framework · Research-informed, not a validated segmentation

Forms of engagement

Design for several forms of engagement. Map the task, reading unit, setting and responsibility before choosing a reader, practice or AI interaction.

Concrete takeaway

Describe the session, not a fixed type of believer. The same person may pray with a short passage, read a whole book and prepare a detailed study. Offer movement between these activities without implying a spiritual ranking.

A spectrum of tasks, not a ladder

These overlapping situations help scope scenarios and recruit a varied research sample. Devotional engagement can be intellectually deep; theological study can be spiritually formative. Time, experience and screen size do not determine the worth of an encounter.

What reading research contributes

Dyer’s 2019 study abstract reports different uses of print and digital media among American evangelical readers, alongside comprehension and frequency differences. These outcomes should be evaluated separately. The full methods were not reviewed for this entry.

In his 2024 discussion, Dyer describes print for devotional and sustained reading, phones for shorter readings and lookup, and desktops for study. He also discusses how study tools and associations with a medium can influence interpretation. These observations are contextual, not universal device preferences.

Neither source compares pericope pages with chapter scrolling. A coherent short unit may help orientation; interruption of narrative continuity may cost understanding. Both are hypotheses for product research.

Aperto: passage-centred design as a hypothesis

Aperto’s design direction uses focused passage pages rather than making long-form scrolling the central encounter. The local prototype models bounded passage references; the mobile prototype also has a shared weekly passage model. Read the evidence scope.

The reusable decision is to select a coherent unit for a chosen practice, retain its full verified text, and let the reader move to the surrounding chapter or continue reading. The weekly model can connect several practices to one passage without making all participants follow the same route.

Test whether this improves orientation, understanding, rereading and voluntary practice for the intended audience. Also measure the cost: hidden context, fragmented narrative, editorial boundary confusion and friction for readers seeking sustained study. Do not convert a preference for short phone sessions into a claim that people cannot or should not read longer passages on a phone.

AI podcasts and source-grounded audio

Google’s Audio Overview documentation describes generated discussions based on selected material, format choices and a deliberate interactive branch. This supplies a product precedent, not evidence of faith-domain effectiveness.

Aperto’s AI podcast direction is recorded as a product-specific application to investigate; its current generation and playback behaviour were not exercised in this review. The general pattern needs an explicit source set, synthetic-voice label, recoverable playback position, transcript and evidence access. A generated overview is interpretation; it must remain distinguishable from an audio Bible recording.

Citations: inspectability before authority

VSB’s captured verse sheet shows numbered markers in an AI summary alongside source video cards and a fallibility label. This establishes visible attribution, not whether each cited source supports its claim. The next evaluation should follow a marker to an excerpt or reliable media anchor, inspect context, and return to the exact claim.

Lumen’s optional engagement choices and verse-aligned comparison add observed interface examples. Its provider statements about grounded answers were not independently validated.

A reusable scenario brief

  1. Name the situation and chosen intent, including a legitimate quiet or no-disclosure route.
  2. Record the passage, edition, boundaries and desired wider context.
  3. Specify device, interruptions, accessibility and any use alongside print.
  4. Describe interpretation, evidence requirements, audience and responsibility.
  5. State the entry, retained anchors, decisions, recovery and a useful endpoint.
  6. Evaluate task success, understanding, agency and lived meaning separately from activity.
