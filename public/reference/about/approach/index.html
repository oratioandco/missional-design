Our approach

Design practice, examined with care.

Learn from established practice. Examine its assumptions. Make a decision that you can explain to the people it affects.

A Christian perspective with room for discernment

This resource considers industry design practice through actual user needs, ethical questions and Christian theological reflection. Established patterns carry assumptions about success, growth, attention and value. Their commercial origins neither make them neutral nor invalidate them. We examine what they reward, who benefits and who carries the costs.

A redemptive frame invites inquiry: what good might this product cultivate, what harm might it repair or reproduce, and whose circumstances are missing? It is not a certification that a product is redemptive. Prayerful discernment belongs alongside research, accessibility, practical testing and accountability.

From situation to considered decision

Begin with the situation and intended good. Name the assumptions, compare alternatives, examine consequences, and make a decision that can be revisited. A default should serve the person’s purpose; usage volume cannot establish spiritual growth. Follow a worked example or use questions and readings for discernment.

Recommendations without a single theological verdict

Charging money can support fair compensation, care and continuity. Different Christian perspectives may emphasise generous access, stewardship, the dignity of paid work or communal provision. Donations, subscriptions, institutional funding and usage-based pricing each introduce dependencies. We compare those consequences and invite a reasoned choice; no funding model is presented as uniquely Christian.

What a useful pattern should contain

  • A recognisable situation and the person’s purpose.
  • An actionable recommendation with credible alternatives.
  • Benefits, drawbacks and different effects across circumstances.
  • A visual appropriate to the decision: a wireframe, sequence, controllable animation, timeline or comparison.
  • Sources with their scope, dates and limits; observation distinguished from interpretation.
  • Questions for reflection and a concrete way to evaluate the choice.

Animations should have static equivalents and respect reduced-motion preferences. Evaluate the design with people using realistic tasks and appropriate outcome measures.

An edited publication that can grow

Editorial readiness and strength of evidence are separate questions. A publishable proposal can still need user research; an established finding can still need careful translation into a different context. Published patterns form an editorially reviewed collection. Further pattern drafts are held back from the catalogue and downloads; their former links lead to related guidance. Research articles remain an evolving reference with source-specific limits.

Patterns marked Published have received an editorial review. In development means the pattern body and illustration are withheld while that review continues. Working drafts are retained outside the public output. Each published page includes practical guidance and its supporting sources.

Questions in development

Builder architecture decisions, Scripture API and licensing comparisons, and starter templates need dedicated investigation. Comparative app-board research will use dated observations and fresh verification where necessary. Provider comparisons and recommendations will follow dedicated research.

Begin with the person’s purpose, explore the design principles, or use the decision worksheet.
