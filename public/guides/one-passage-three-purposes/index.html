Purpose before pattern

One passage, three purposes

A useful interface begins with what someone is trying to do. Compare three briefs for Luke 15:1–7—the same passage, with different reasons to open it.

These are illustrative design briefs, not findings from a user study. The same person could move between all three. Each brief offers a starting decision to test, rather than a universal rule.

Receive a shared passage · Prepare a group conversation · Investigate an interpretation

01 · Illustrative brief

Receive a shared passage

“A friend sent me this. I want to read it and consider why it matters.”

  1. Open Luke 15:1–7
  2. Read or listen
  3. Reflect, explore or finish

A starting decision

Open the passage directly, with the translation named and an easy route to its wider context. Give the passage a visible ending. Keep listening and text-size controls within reach.

The assumption to examine

A bounded passage can make beginning manageable. It also embodies an editorial choice about where meaning begins and ends. Name that boundary; let the reader read before and after it.

When to choose differently

A chapter-first reader may better serve someone following along elsewhere. A guided question may help a newcomer, but should not require an answer or assume personal disclosure.

Agency and a meaningful ending

Avoid turning the ending into an automatic next devotion, streak prompt or chat invitation. Offer “Read the surrounding passage” when it helps; leaving can be a successful outcome.

A realistic test

Ask someone unfamiliar with this interface to open the shared link, identify the edition, find the surrounding text and finish. Can they explain what was Scripture and what was commentary?

02 · Illustrative brief

Prepare a group conversation

“We will discuss this passage together. I need to prepare, then find my place quickly.”

  1. Open the group passage
  2. Read and prepare notes
  3. Return during discussion

A starting decision

Keep the agreed passage stable. Offer discreet verse anchors, personal notes and a reliable return after opening a cross-reference. Make note privacy explicit.

The assumption to examine

The organiser’s selection gives the group a shared starting point; it can also give one person substantial control over the questions and interpretation. Preparation and participation should not require public progress.

When to choose differently

For live follow-along, a quick book/chapter picker may matter more than a resource panel. A newcomer may need help finding the passage; a facilitator may need several sources. Offer these routes without assigning permanent roles from usage history.

Agency and a meaningful ending

Keep “my preparation” separate from “share with the group.” Do not publish a private question, completion state or prayer request as the default outcome of preparing.

A realistic test

Rehearse opening a second passage and returning while someone is speaking. Then ask participants to explain which notes others can see. Include a person joining without preparation.

03 · Illustrative brief

Investigate an interpretation

“I want to understand a disputed reading before teaching or forming a conclusion.”

  1. Frame the question
  2. Compare text and sources
  3. Record a provisional conclusion

A starting decision

Offer comparison, inspectable sources and notes that preserve reference and edition. Keep the original question and a return path visible as the reader follows evidence.

The assumption to examine

More available resources do not guarantee greater understanding. A default commentary, translation or AI summary can privilege a tradition while appearing neutral. Make provenance and editorial selection discoverable.

When to choose differently

Side-by-side panes can help on a wide screen; verse-by-verse comparison may work better on a phone. Interlinear detail answers some language questions but is not a substitute for contextual interpretation.

Agency and a meaningful ending

Allow sustained investigation without an imposed devotional ending. Also make it possible to stop and record what remains uncertain. An application prompt should invite reflection, not force a moral lesson from every research session.

A realistic test

Ask a reader to compare two interpretations, inspect what supports one claim, and return to the original question. Observe lost context and mistaken attribution; do not use time spent as evidence of insight.

What changes—and what remains

The passage is unchanged. The prominence of navigation, notes, comparison, guidance and the next invitation changes with the task. Accessibility, accurate attribution, reader control and a route to context remain important in each brief.

Is activity standing in for faithfulness?

In this resource, performance spirituality names the risk of treating frequency, volume, completion or consistency as proof of faithfulness or spiritual growth. It does not mean that daily reading, sustained study or an annual Bible plan is wrong. These can be freely chosen and meaningful disciplines. The design question is whether one practice becomes an unexamined requirement for everyone.

A completion badge, an uninterrupted reading surface and a single-passage page each carry assumptions. None proves its reader has grown spiritually. Ask what this product intends to support, whether the person shares that intention, and what experience would challenge the design.

Take this into a product review

  1. Name a real situation and the good the product is intended to serve.
  2. Identify what the default encourages, makes difficult or leaves out.
  3. Compare a credible alternative—including a quieter or smaller intervention.
  4. State what people can choose, decline, change or finish.
  5. Record the evidence, theological commitments and unresolved judgments separately.
  6. Test the practical consequences with people in the situation, including those who opt out.
