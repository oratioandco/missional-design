Start from a purpose

Start with the person’s purpose. Choose the situation closest to your product decision. These are overlapping purposes, not types of people or stages of spiritual maturity.

The same person may listen during a commute, follow a guided reflection in the evening and prepare to teach at the weekend. Receiving, following guidance and directing one’s own reading can each serve a meaningful purpose. More interaction is not automatically better engagement.

See the same passage designed for three purposes. Compare the assumptions before choosing a pattern.

Before choosing: consider familiarity with Scripture, language and literacy, disability and access needs, available time, connectivity, emotional circumstances and whether the device is shared. These conditions can change which option helps.

Receive and listen

A commute, a rest or a hands-free encounter with Scripture.

Reduce visual demands while preserving control over pace, repetition and stopping. Listening is not a lesser form of engagement.

When the purpose changes

Compare daily offerings, bounded plans and self-directed reading. Content selection, commitment, calendar and progress are separate design choices.

Let a listener open the text, a guided reader explore wider context, and a researcher return to quiet reading without losing their place. Do not infer a permanent persona from one session.
