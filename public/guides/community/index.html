Community and visibility

Who can see this, respond to it, and hold authority?

A shared reading plan, a prayer group, a public comment thread and a mentoring relationship can all be called “community”. They have different audiences, different risks and different people responsible when something goes wrong. More visible activity is not proof that anyone belongs.

01

Begin with the relationship, not the feed

Name the relationship the feature supports, then decide who sees what, who can respond, and who has authority. Online relationships can be meaningful and accessible in their own right; sending people “offline” is not always possible, or safe.

02

Show the audience before anything is shared

Let people choose what to share, with private notes and anything the AI inferred left out unless they add it deliberately. Preview the exact audience and content, including anyone named in it, and confirm with a clear visibility state. Make report, mute, block and leave easy to find, say what group administrators can see, and be honest that removal has limits once someone else has copied the material.

03

Say what can be removed, before people join

YouVersion’s Android help distinguishes private plans, friends-only plan activity in the Community Feed, and Plan with Friends activity visible to participants. It also says Community Feed and timeline plan activity cannot be deleted. That is documented behaviour, not a tested current flow, but the lesson holds: show who will see someone’s participation before they join, and what they can take back.

A block button is not a moderation service. eSafety’s Safety by Design framework places the provider’s responsibility alongside users’ autonomy and accountability.

Take into your next review

Can someone decline to share, miss a session, challenge a leader or leave the group without a public penalty?

Exercise

Walk through each of those moments with the people who would use the feature, and with whoever would moderate it. Write down who handles a report on a Sunday evening.

What still needs investigation

Use these initial proposals to plan research with participants and moderators. Include the needs of minors, your community’s traditions and the relationships you hope to support over time. Define moderation and safeguarding responsibilities before launching a service.

The Kairos manuscript argues for formation connected to community and embodied action. That is a theological contribution, not evidence that a particular social feature works.
