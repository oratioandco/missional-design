Missional by Design · The practice

Make room for rest, and say when it is enough

A faithful product sometimes asks for less: it ends a session, keeps the night quiet, stops asking when the need is met, and lets itself end when its work is done.

From the calling

  • Leave room for rest
  • Keep a sabbath from shipping

Scripture and tradition

“The Sabbath was made for man, not man for the Sabbath” (Mark 2:27). When the people kept bringing offerings for the tabernacle Bezalel built, Moses “sent a proclamation throughout the camp”, and “the people were restrained from bringing more, since what they already had was more than enough to perform all the work” (Exodus 36:6–7).

The Rule of Benedict keeps silence especially at night. FaithTech’s practice of stewardship includes “choosing not to release a product or allowing one to end”.

Sources: The Rule of Benedict · The FaithTech Playbook

What it rules in, and out

In

  • Sessions that end in silence instead of autoplay
  • Quiet hours; after silence, ask once and stop
  • An appeal that says when the need is met
  • A regular gift or subscription as easy to stop as to start

Out

  • An endless feed of devotional content
  • Reminders that multiply when someone stops answering
  • Asking again and again

What the maker gives up. Time in the app, and the comfort of a product that never has to end.

Qualification. Some people want more: a long listen, a full plan. Enough is the person’s to choose; the product’s part is to offer an end and keep it.

Test a design

Where does our product stop, and does it ever tell someone they have done enough?
