Missional by Design · The practice

Feed the flock, not on it

Attention, time, devotion and money are entrusted, not harvested. Ask for them honestly, never through guilt, fear, urgency or a promise.

From the calling

  • The burden: shepherds who feed themselves
  • Attention is entrusted, not harvested

Scripture and tradition

“Woe to the shepherds of Israel, who only feed themselves! Should not the shepherds feed their flock?” (Ezekiel 34:2). “Feed My sheep” (John 21:17). “Each one should give what he has decided in his heart to give, not out of regret or compulsion” (2 Corinthians 9:7).

Praxis asks builders to make “what people want to want”, instead of products that appeal to “our most self-centered, lonely, addictive tendencies”. FaithTech names the tools of the reckless posture: “Dopamine loops, infinite scrolling, doomscrolling, echo-chamber, and engineered dependencies”. faith.tools asks that any cost be “very clear”.

Sources: The Redemptive Business playbook: products · The FaithTech Playbook · faith.tools selection criteria

What it rules in, and out

In

  • Reminders the person sets; streaks that can be switched off; a return without shame
  • Gifts that start at no amount; prices that say what they pay for
  • An honest free allowance, and a subscription that is easy to end

Out

  • Broken-streak warnings marked urgent
  • “We miss you” in a persona’s voice
  • Preselected tiers and added tips; asks after a prayer request
  • Weekly plans, hidden renewals and hard cancellation

What the maker gives up. The daily users, conversions and revenue that pressure would bring.

Qualification. Asking is not wrong: the church has always supported those who serve the word, and a chosen rhythm can help. The line is crossed when the ask uses guilt, fear, a moment of need or God’s name.

Test a design

Does this serve growth in faith, or only the usage numbers?

  • Freedom or anxiety?
  • Would someone who was short of money, or grieving, feel pressed by this screen?
