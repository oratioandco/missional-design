Missional by Design · The people

Plant and water; leave the growth to God

Measure what serves the person and the task. Never score a soul, and never claim the fruit as yours.

From the calling

  • We plant and water; God gives the growth
  • The app is a means, not the destination

Scripture and tradition

“Neither he who plants nor he who waters is anything, but only God, who makes things grow” (1 Corinthians 3:7). The seed “sprouts and grows, though he knows not how” (Mark 4:27). “So My word that proceeds from My mouth will not return to Me empty” (Isaiah 55:11).

Dallas Willard distinguished effort from earning. FaithTech warns of the Killer App Trap, “the belief that technology will solve our deepest problems”.

Sources: Spiritual Disciplines and Means of Grace: Contrast or Continuum? · The FaithTech Playbook

What it rules in, and out

In

  • Honest evaluation of usability, understanding, burden and harm
  • Accounts people choose to give
  • Saying what the product does not know

Out

  • Faith scores and maturity levels inferred from use
  • Global prayer counters as achievements
  • Usage presented as spiritual fruit
  • “Life-changing” claims without evidence

What the maker gives up. The impressive number for the pitch.

Qualification. Humility is not a reason to avoid evaluation. Measure usability, safety and understanding carefully; it is spiritual maturity that no telemetry can show.

Test a design

What would we stop measuring if we believed growth is God’s?

  • Will the person’s motivation survive removing the feature?
