Habits and spiritual formation

Support meaningful practice while preserving autonomy.

These patterns are hypotheses and ethical design proposals. Their effects on actual reading practices, motivation and spiritual formation need research rather than assumptions from engagement metrics.

These cards are proposed hypotheses. Read the research agenda and study integration method.

Research-informed design proposal105 · Habits and spiritual formation

Reading plans

From a plan tied to calendar dates, through a sequence that waits for the reader and a weekly rhythm, to readings that belong to each day, and streaks as a warning case: decide what a plan asks of time, and what happens when someone comes back after days away.

Research-informed design proposal106 · Habits and spiritual formation

Prayer

From the received words of a church’s daily office, through guided steps and a list of people to pray for, to quiet prayer with nothing to fill in, and counted prayer as a warning case: decide what each surface asks someone to read, type, share or count.

Research-informed design proposal108 · Habits and spiritual formation

Devotional audio

From guided prayer with Scripture read twice and room for silence, through a teaching conversation and a one-voice devotional talk, to a sleep story that fades out, and an endless generated stream as a warning case: decide what each format does with Scripture, with the listener’s attention, and with the end of the session.

Research-informed design proposal107 · Habits and spiritual formation

Check-ins and assessments

From a few setup questions that only shape what the app suggests, through a daily check-in, a gifts inventory and a church survey, to a spiritual score as a warning case: decide what each question is for, what is kept, who sees a result, and how much the result claims to know.

Research-informed design proposal110 · Habits and spiritual formation

Sharing with a group

From a request kept between someone and God, through one listener they choose, a few friends and a group with a leader, to the whole church and a public wall, with a board ranked by prayer streaks as a warning case: decide who hears a request, what each listener can and must do with it, and what comes back.

Research-informed design proposal · draft for review111 · Habits and spiritual formation

Reading together

From the same passage on the same day with nothing shown, through a guide for meeting in person, a group with a leader and a plan with friends, to a feed of friends’ activity and a public thread on the verse: decide what the others see of each person’s reading, and who looks after what is said.

Research-informed design proposal · draft for review112 · Habits and spiritual formation

First use

From opening on a passage, a prayer or a question box with nothing asked, through a first page of places to start, guest use and a short tour, to questions first and payment before the first use: decide what a new person must give, and when, before they reach what they came for.

Research-informed design proposal · draft for review113 · Habits and spiritual formation

Children and families

From a story read aloud with a grown-up, through family time in a general app, a children’s Bible app and games with episodes, to a youth group app with a leader and a child alone with an AI or a public community: decide who is with the child, what the app collects, how it holds attention, and what happens when a child says something worrying.

Research-informed design proposal · draft for review114 · Habits and spiritual formation

Notifications and reminders

From an app that waits to be opened, through reminders the person sets, the church’s hours of prayer, a daily word and messages from people, to notifications tuned to bring people back and an AI that writes first: decide who chooses the interruption, how urgent it claims to be, what it says to someone who has been away, and what reaches someone at night.

Research-informed design proposal · draft for review115 · Habits and spiritual formation

Giving and offerings

From leaving giving to the church, through a quiet way to give, the church’s offering in the app, a regular gift and showing what a gift does, to a gift tied to an ad-free app and, as the warning case, pressure and promises: decide where the ask appears, what is chosen for the giver, and what the app says a gift will do.
